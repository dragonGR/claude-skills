# Greek writers, English text and Greek text

Read this when editing English written by a Greek speaker (the owner of this plugin is one), when writing to Greek clients, or when writing or cleaning up Greek prose.

## Editing non-native English

Fix what a reader would misread, and errors a reader would notice as errors. Leave everything else. The author's sentence structure, directness and vocabulary are their voice; "improving" them into polished corporate English makes the text longer, vaguer and less like the person who signs it.

### Fix: errors that change the meaning

| Written | Usually means | Write |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Send it until Friday" (μέχρι) | a deadline | "by Friday" ("until" means the action continues up to Friday) |
| "The site is down since three days" (εδώ και) | duration | "for three days", or "since Monday" |
| "I work here since 2019" | a state that continues | "I have worked here since 2019" |
| "He is very sympathetic" (συμπαθητικός) | likeable | "likeable", "easy to work with" |
| "Close the server", "open the PC" (κλείνω, ανοίγω) | power off, power on | "shut down", "turn on"; in tech text ask, because "close" can also mean a connection or a port |
| "1.500 €", "2,5%" | fifteen hundred euros, two and a half percent | "€1,500", "2.5%" in English text for international readers; keep the Greek format only if the whole document uses it, and never mix the two |
| "05/10/2026" | 5 October | "5 October 2026" or `2026-10-05`; a US reader reads May 10 |

When "possibly" or "probably" carries weight (an incident cause, a delivery estimate), check which confidence the author means before editing around it.

### Fix lightly: grammar that readers notice

Change the words that are wrong and nothing around them.

- Uncountable nouns in the plural: "informations", "feedbacks", "advices", "equipments", "softwares" become "information", "feedback", "advice", "equipment", "software".
- Extra or missing prepositions: "discuss about" becomes "discuss", "explain me" becomes "explain to me", "waiting your answer" becomes "waiting for your answer", "return back" becomes "return".
- Articles on general or abstract nouns: "The security is important for us" becomes "Security is important to us".
- "Make a question" becomes "ask a question". "Take a decision" is correct English; leave it.
- Long sentences joined with commas, which Greek tolerates and English reads as run-ons: split them, or use a colon or semicolon where one clause explains the other.

### Leave alone

- Direct requests. "Please send me the contract by Friday" is polite English. It does not need "Would you mind kindly sharing the contract at your earliest convenience".
- "Good morning" or "Καλημέρα" as an opener to a Greek client, and "Kind regards" or "Thanks" as a closing.
- Word order that is grammatical but not what a native would pick: "Tomorrow we will deploy the fix."
- The author's plain words. Do not upgrade "problem" to "challenge", "fix" to "remediate", "big" to "significant", "check" to "review".
- British or American spelling, whichever the author uses.
- Never add idioms the author does not use: "circle back", "touch base", "reach out", "loop in", "ballpark", "on my radar", "bandwidth", "at your earliest convenience".

### Example

The author's draft:

> Hi Nikos,
>
> The new version is ready in staging. Please check it until Wednesday so we can go live on Thursday. We made the changes you asked in the checkout, the only thing missing is the invoice PDF, we are waiting the template from your accountant. If you have any feedbacks send them to me directly.
>
> Thanks,
> Alex

Over-corrected (wrong):

> Hi Nikos, I hope you're doing well! I'm pleased to let you know that the new version is now live on our staging environment. We'd greatly appreciate it if you could take a moment to review it by Wednesday, allowing us to proceed with the Thursday launch…

Right:

> Hi Nikos,
>
> The new version is ready in staging. Please check it by Wednesday so we can go live on Thursday. We made the checkout changes you asked for. The only thing missing is the invoice PDF: we are waiting for the template from your accountant. If you have any feedback, send it to me directly.
>
> Thanks,
> Alex

The edits: "until" to "by" (a deadline, not a duration), the run-on sentence split at the colon, "waiting the" to "waiting for the", "feedbacks… them" to "feedback… it", and "the changes you asked in the checkout" to "the checkout changes you asked for". The directness, the length and the sign-off stay, because they are Alex's.

## Writing Greek

Generated Greek has the same habits as generated English, often as direct calques.

| Tell | Write instead |
| --- | --- |
| «Στον σημερινό ψηφιακό κόσμο», «Σε ένα διαρκώς μεταβαλλόμενο περιβάλλον» | cut the clause |
| «Αξίζει να σημειωθεί ότι», «Είναι σημαντικό να τονιστεί ότι» | cut; start with the fact |
| «Επιπλέον», «Επιπροσθέτως», «Παράλληλα» at the start of every other sentence | the real relation («γιατί», «οπότε», «αλλά») or nothing |
| «Εν κατακλείδι», «Συμπερασματικά», «Συνοψίζοντας» | cut the summary paragraph |
| «διαδραματίζει καθοριστικό/κρίσιμο ρόλο» | say what it does |
| «αποτελεί», «λειτουργεί ως» used everywhere | «είναι» |
| «όχι μόνο… αλλά και» | «και», or two sentences |
| participial tails: «, διασφαλίζοντας…», «, ενισχύοντας…», «, αναδεικνύοντας…», «, επιτρέποντας…» | cut, or a separate claim with its mechanism |
| «ολιστική προσέγγιση», «απρόσκοπτη εμπειρία», «καινοτόμες λύσεις», «εύρωστο», «βελτιστοποιημένο» | the fact the adjective stands in for |
| calques: «κάνει νόημα», «στο τέλος της ημέρας», «είμαστε ενθουσιασμένοι να ανακοινώσουμε», «ξεκλειδώστε», «το ταξίδι σας» | «βγάζει νόημα» or «έχει λογική», «τελικά», «ανακοινώνουμε», the actual action, «η διαδικασία» |

Example. Draft:

> Στον σημερινό ψηφιακό κόσμο, η ασφάλεια των δεδομένων διαδραματίζει καθοριστικό ρόλο. Η πλατφόρμα μας αποτελεί μια ολιστική λύση που όχι μόνο προστατεύει τα δεδομένα σας, αλλά και βελτιστοποιεί την απόδοση, διασφαλίζοντας μια απρόσκοπτη εμπειρία. Εν κατακλείδι, πρόκειται για ένα καινοτόμο εργαλείο.

With the facts from the author (daily encrypted backups, 30 days kept, monthly restore test):

> Παίρνουμε κρυπτογραφημένα αντίγραφα ασφαλείας κάθε μέρα και τα κρατάμε 30 ημέρες. Μία φορά τον μήνα δοκιμάζουμε την επαναφορά.

The claim about performance had no fact behind it and is gone.

### Greek conventions to keep

- The Greek question mark is «;». Do not change it to «?». (Unicode has a separate GREEK QUESTION MARK, U+037E, which normalizes to the ordinary semicolon, so most text simply uses «;».)
- The ano teleia «·» is the Greek semicolon.
- Quotation marks are «» in most Greek text; keep whatever the document uses.
- Text in capitals drops the tonos («ΠΡΟΣΟΧΗ», not «ΠΡΟΣΟΧΉ») and keeps the diaeresis («ΠΡΩΤΕΪΝΗ»). Where the tonos was what kept two vowels apart, capitals add a diaeresis the lowercase word lacks: «Μάιος» becomes «ΜΑΪΟΣ», «τσάι» becomes «ΤΣΑΪ». The disjunctive «ή» keeps its accent («Ή»). When code produces the capitals, plain uppercasing gets both rules wrong: in Node 26 `'Μάιος'.toUpperCase()` returns «ΜΆΙΟΣ», and Python's `'Μάιος'.upper()` does the same. Use `toLocaleUpperCase('el')` in JavaScript, or ICU's Greek-locale uppercasing elsewhere. CSS `text-transform: uppercase` depends on the browser and the page's `lang`, so check the rendered text.
- Headings in sentence case. Title case is not a Greek convention at all.
- Avoid the em dash in Greek technical prose too; commas, parentheses or the ano teleia do the job.
- Formality: πληθυντικός ευγενείας (εσείς) with clients unless the thread already uses εσύ, and do not switch in the middle of a thread.
- Engineering vocabulary: Greek teams write «το PR», «κάνω deploy», «το branch», «το API». Keep English terms in Latin script where the team does, and do not force translations such as «κλάδος» for branch that nobody on the team uses.
- Greeklish: fine to read in chat, never to produce in a document unless asked.
